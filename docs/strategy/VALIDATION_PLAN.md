# VALIDATION_PLAN

## Hypothesis

Solo SEO consultants and micro-agencies will pay a small monthly subscription for a recurring SEO + AI/search visibility action brief if it is cheaper and more focused than broad SEO suites.

Near-term research hypothesis:

Solo SEO consultants and micro-agencies have repeated friction around turning Search Console, rank tracking, and client reporting data into a clear monthly update with the next action.

## Segment

Primary ICP:

Solo SEO consultants and micro-agencies managing 3-20 small client sites.

First validation wedge:

Local SEO consultants and micro-agencies managing small-business or local/service-business client sites. Use the broader ICP as backup, but send the next outreach batch to local-first prospects because their AI/search visibility checks can be tied to service, city, competitors, GBP, reviews, directories, and local source gaps.

Best-fit early customer:

- Already uses GSC.
- Has at least 2 active SEO clients.
- Has considered or canceled Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking, Ubersuggest, LowFruits, or BrightLocal.
- Needs monthly client reporting or prioritization.
- Charges enough per client to absorb or pass through `$49/site/mo`; ultra-low-retainer operators are likely research contacts before paid prospects.

## Offer

DuckRadar: `$49/site/mo`.

One subscribed site receives one monthly client-ready action brief: a short decision memo explaining what changed, why it matters, and what to do next.

## Experiment

Revenue-first validation.

The original two-stage validation was:

- Stage 1: Mom Test style workflow discovery.
- Stage 2: paid subscription validation after repeated pain is visible.

As of 2026-06-17, this is too passive. Cold research outreach has not produced conversations or revenue quickly enough.

The current experiment is a one-week paid pilot sprint:

- Use Mom Test questions to qualify real workflow pain.
- Ask directly for a `$49` first month when a prospect is warm, referred, engaged, or has described a real current GSC/reporting workflow.
- Prioritize live conversations, warm intros, social/form outreach, and paid asks over more market research.

Actions:

1. Build a list of 50 target consultants or micro-agencies.
2. Send warm intro requests asking for consultants or agency owners who hate monthly reporting.
3. Send direct workflow questions to high-fit consultants and micro-agency owners.
4. Use workflow questions to understand the last real monthly update they prepared.
5. Log current workflow, tools used, frequency, pain, workaround, and exact language.
6. Ask for the paid pilot and collect payment through Paddle when the prospect has a real use case and agrees to try one site.
7. Deliver the first monthly brief manually.
8. Ask if they want to add another site after seeing the first brief.

## Pricing Test

Primary price to present:

- `$49/site/mo`.

Do not change the public price for this wedge. On calls only, ask whether a 3-site agency bundle around `$129-$149/mo` would be easier to buy than per-site pricing.

Discovery questions to collect pricing data:

- At what price would this feel too expensive for one client site?
- At what price would this feel suspiciously cheap?
- What would you pay for 3 sites?
- Would you rather pay per site or one agency bundle?
- Would you pass this through to clients or absorb it as tooling cost?

Do not present multiple public prices in the first test. Multiple prices will make the early signal harder to read.

## Metrics

Primary metric:

- High signal workflow conversations.

Secondary metrics:

- Number of prospects describing a recent real workflow.
- Number of repeated pains across at least 3 prospects.
- Number of prospects asking to see DuckRadar after describing workflow pain.
- Outreach-to-call rate.
- Call-to-paid-subscription rate.
- Paid subscriptions.
- First-report retention.
- Sites per customer.
- Manual fulfillment time per site.
- Requests for additional sites.

Guardrail metrics:

- Number of prospects asking for features outside scope.
- Number of prospects rejecting because price is still too high.
- Number of customers unable to provide GSC access.
- Paddle checkout visits vs. completed subscriptions.

## Success Criteria

One-week revenue sprint success by 2026-06-24:

- At least 1 paid customer starts.
- Or at least 3 prospects ask to see a sample, checkout, or input requirements after describing a real workflow.
- Or at least 5 live conversations reveal the same reporting pain.

Original research stage success:

- 15 workflow conversations in 30 days.
- 5 high signal replies or calls with recent workflow details.
- 3 repeated pains across independent prospects.
- 3 prospects ask to see the product, sample, or report after discussing workflow.

Paid stage success:

- 10 paid subscriptions in 30 days after the research pattern is clear.
- 5 subscribers remain after first report.
- 3 customers add or ask about a second site.
- 30-minute or lower monthly fulfillment time after first report.
- At least 5 customers say the brief helps them avoid another SEO tool or reduces tool dependence.

## Kill / Pivot Criteria

Kill or rethink if:

- 20 direct paid pilot asks produce zero serious conversations.
- Fewer than 5 real workflow conversations after 50 targeted outreaches.
- Prospects mostly give polite opinions but no current workflow pain.
- No pain repeats across at least 3 independent prospects.
- Fewer than 3 paid subscriptions after a focused paid offer is sent to prospects with confirmed pain.
- Most prospects want free scans but refuse `$49/mo`.
- Customers do not open/use the brief.
- Customers cannot act on the recommendations.
- Fulfillment stays above 60 minutes/site/month after process tightening.

Pivot if:

- Consultants want an agency bundle more than per-site pricing.
- Customers want a one-time audit but not recurring monitoring.
- B2B SaaS or local SEO specialists show stronger willingness to pay.
- AI visibility is the hook, but GSC opportunities are the retained value.
- Local SEO specialists reject AI visibility as noisy or performative after 20 targeted sends, while broader SEO consultants still want the GSC action brief.
