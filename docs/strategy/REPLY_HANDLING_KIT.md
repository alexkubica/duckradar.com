# DuckRadar Reply Handling Kit

Last updated: 2026-06-14

Use this after research-first outreach. The goal is to keep replies focused on the prospect's real workflow before pitching DuckRadar.

## Core Rule

Do not pitch the subscription until the prospect describes a current workflow, repeated pain, or workaround.

Log every useful reply in `docs/strategy/USER_RESEARCH_LOG.md` with:

- Current workflow
- Tools used
- Frequency
- Pain
- Existing workaround
- Budget or data access signal
- Next question
- Pitch timing

## Target Criteria

Best fit:

- Solo SEO consultant or micro-agency.
- Manages 3 to 20 client sites.
- Sends recurring SEO updates, reports, or prioritization notes.
- Uses GSC, GA4, Looker Studio, Semrush, Ahrefs, SE Ranking, BrightLocal, Screaming Frog, or similar.
- Works with local, service-business, niche-content, or smaller B2B clients.
- Has enough client margin to absorb or pass through `$49/site/mo`.
- Can share GSC read-only access or CSV exports.

Avoid:

- Prospects who only want free audits.
- Agencies already selling a close DuckRadar-style report.
- Very low-retainer operators where `$49/site/mo` is too high.
- Anyone asking for ranking guarantees or ChatGPT visibility guarantees.

## Reply Scripts

### Curious Or Yes

```text
Thanks, useful to hear.

Before I explain too much, how are you handling that workflow today?

Mostly trying to understand what actually happens when you put together a monthly update for a smaller client.
```

### What Is DuckRadar?

```text
DuckRadar helps turn Search Console data into a clearer monthly client update draft.

The main thing I am testing is whether consultants actually need help deciding the next client action, not just another dashboard.

How do you currently make that decision for smaller clients?
```

### Too Busy

```text
Totally fair.

One quick question is enough if you have 30 seconds.

When you send monthly SEO updates, what part usually takes the most manual judgment?
```

### Already Uses Dashboards

```text
Makes sense.

What I am trying to understand is what happens after the dashboard.

Do you usually send clients the dashboard directly, or do you still turn it into a written update with next actions?
```

### AI Visibility Is Vanity

```text
I mostly agree when it is sold as magic.

The useful part, if any, seems to be a small directional check alongside Search Console, not a promise.

In your client reporting, does AI search come up at all, or do you ignore it unless clients ask?
```

### Asks For Sample

```text
Yes, I can send the current sample.

Before I do, what would you want to compare it against?

Your current client update, a Looker report, an audit template, or something else?
```

If they answer with workflow detail:

```text
That helps. Here is the sample:

[sample link]

Main thing I would value is where it misses compared with how you actually report to clients.
```

### Asks Price

```text
The current test price is $49 per site per month.

I am not trying to force that before understanding fit though.

Would this come out of your tooling budget, or would you pass it through to a client?
```

### Not Interested

```text
No problem.

Useful to know.

Is that because the workflow is already solved, the problem is not important, or the format is wrong?
```

### Asks To Unsubscribe

```text
Understood. I will not contact you again.

Thanks.
```

### Wants A Call

```text
Happy to.

Best format is 15 minutes, mostly me asking how you handle monthly updates today.

No full demo unless it turns out the workflow is relevant.

What is a good day this week?
```

## Five Question Discovery Call Script

1. For one smaller client you updated recently, can you walk me through how you created the monthly SEO update?
2. What tools or templates did you open, and which parts still needed manual judgment?
3. What part took longer than it should, got skipped, or was hard to explain to the client?
4. How do you use Search Console when deciding the next action, especially when the data is messy?
5. What are you already paying for or manually doing to make this workflow easier?

Close only after real pain:

```text
Based on what you described, DuckRadar might fit one site.

The current offer is one monthly client-ready brief from GSC data for $49 per site per month.

Want to try it for one client site and judge it against your next monthly update?
```

## Qualification Rubric

High fit:

- Manages multiple client sites.
- Sends monthly updates.
- Uses GSC but still does manual prioritization.
- Names a recent painful workflow.
- Has budget or can pass through `$49/site/mo`.
- Can provide GSC access or exports.
- Asks for sample, price, or next step after describing pain.

Next step: offer one paid site.

Medium fit:

- Real workflow exists, but pain or urgency is unclear.
- Uses dashboards but still may need written interpretation.
- Interested in sample but does not describe a current problem.
- Price sensitivity unknown.

Next step: ask one more workflow or budget question.

Low fit:

- Gives compliments only.
- Talks hypothetically.
- Wants a free audit.
- No repeated monthly reporting workflow.
- Cannot provide data.
- Treats AI visibility as the whole product.

Next step: close politely or keep as research only.

Disqualify:

- Unsubscribes.
- Wants ranking or AI visibility guarantees.
- Already sells the same thing.
- Cannot access client data.
- Only wants unpaid consulting.
